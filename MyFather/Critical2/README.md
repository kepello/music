_Critical (2)_

“Critical” was written by my friend A’shon, who asked me to arrange his poem as a song. His words are built around Paul’s description at 2 Timothy 3:1–5 of the conditions that would exist during “the last days,” and one of the things I appreciated immediately was what he did with the word critical. We use that word pretty casually, but in a hospital an announcement that someone is in critical condition means something very specific and very serious. A’shon takes Paul’s description of “critical times hard to deal with” and carries that medical imagery throughout the song: ICU spiritual, system flatline, diagnosis critical. The world isn’t simply having a difficult period in his telling of it; it is a patient showing the symptoms of a condition Paul described long ago.

I think that imagery resonated with me because I feel many of those symptoms around us now, and from A’shon’s words it seems clear that he does too. The song works its way through Paul’s list without simply reciting it: love of self and money, disobedience, lack of natural affection, disloyalty, slander, lack of self-control, fierceness, pride and an outward appearance of godliness without its power. A’shon translates those things into images from the world he sees around him, sometimes serious and painful and sometimes with clever wordplay that makes you stop for a second before realizing what he has done. Underneath all of it, though, the song isn’t hopeless. A critical diagnosis matters because it tells you what is wrong, and in this case the diagnosis also points toward the cure. “But I hold faith, yeah that’s pivotal / Jeh the healer, cure is biblical.”

That’s one of the things I have enjoyed about putting someone else’s words to music. I didn’t write “Critical,” so I approached it differently than I do my own lyrics. My part was to listen for what was already in A’shon’s words and find music that let me hear it more clearly. In the process, his poem introduced me to a style of music I might not otherwise have explored, while his imagery gave me a different way to think about a scripture I already knew very well.

The situation is critical, but that isn’t the same thing as hopeless. The symptoms were written down long ago, and so was the cure.
