_The Words that Remain_

I’ve read the account of Hannah before, but I hadn’t thought about what it meant for her to keep going to Shiloh. Peninnah’s provoking was part of Hannah’s home life, and the account describes her making the journey to worship year after year. She could have stayed back from the family strain, but she went. What moves me is that she didn’t wait for life to become peaceful before approaching Jehovah.

The account doesn’t tell us what Hannah shared with Elkanah or why she may not have asked him to intervene. I found myself wondering whether she feared that his involvement might make things harder, but that’s only a possibility. What is clear is that she brought her distress to Jehovah herself.

Then Eli, who should have recognized a worshipper in pain, misjudged her. He saw her lips moving silently and accused her of being drunk. That could have hurt deeply. Hannah answered him calmly and explained, and Eli changed his view—but I can understand why that moment might have added to her burden.

What I hadn’t noticed before is the difference between the peace she found that day and the joy that returned over time. The account shows her leaving with peace, while her joy bloomed again across the months. That feels realistic to me: relief can begin before healing is complete. Hannah’s faith didn’t erase the strain in her family, but it helped her keep worshipping through it. And the prayer she poured out there is still here for us to read—words that remain.
