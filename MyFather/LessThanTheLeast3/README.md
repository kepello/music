_Less Than the Least (3)_

After making two very different arrangements of this song, I started wondering whether I could bring together what I liked about both of them. The first had the energy and rhythm that made the words stay in my head, while the second had an acoustic simplicity and exposure that made me hear those same words differently. Could I make something upbeat and percussive without losing that simplicity? That question became this third version.

It landed somewhere around folk rock, with modal acoustic textures, a powerful frame drum and an energy that feels to me almost like an old pilgrim song. It has enough rhythmic drive to carry the words forward but still feels fundamentally acoustic and uncomplicated. Of the three versions, this is probably the one I will now work toward actually playing myself on acoustic guitar. It suits my musical taste and the way I like to play, but it is also a stretch for me, as quite a few of the songs I write turn out to be.

That has become an interesting part of making music this way. I use AI extensively to help me express thoughts musically that I don’t yet have the ability to fully express on my own. I can hear possibilities beyond what I can currently play, and instead of that being the end of the process, I increasingly find myself wanting to stretch my own playing until I can play the music I’ve written. In a strange way, the finished song can become less of a demonstration of what I can do and more of a picture of something I would like to become capable of doing.

That seems particularly appropriate for this song because Paul’s example feels much the same way to me. His humility is powerful and moving, and I can see the lesson clearly enough to know that I want it, but seeing it and living it as deeply as he did are very different things. That is a much greater stretch than learning a difficult guitar part, but the idea of having something worthwhile beyond where I am now and deliberately reaching toward it feels very similar.

So this version has become personal to me in a way I didn’t really anticipate when I first heard that passing comment in a Bible discourse about taking off the old personality and putting on the new one. I hope I eventually learn to play this song myself. Much more importantly, I hope I keep learning to live what I wrote in it.

May we learn, like Paul before us, to be less than the least.
