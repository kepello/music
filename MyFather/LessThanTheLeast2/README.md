_Less Than the Least (2)_

After finishing the first version, I found that I wasn’t really finished with the song. Paul’s example had stuck with me and resonated on several different levels. Some aspects of his transformation are enormous and impossible to miss, but others are much more subtle and become apparent gradually in the way he talks about himself. I wanted to hear the same words from another angle, and rather than making the message memorable through rhythm and energy, I wondered what would happen if I stripped almost everything away from it.

This version is much more exposed: two acoustic guitars, one playing a punctuated, syncopated rhythm and the other playing lead, with an aged male voice that sounds mournful, fragile and at times nearly on the edge of crying. There is deliberately very little production around it—no compression, reverb, echo or delay to smooth things out or make the performance prettier. I wanted it to sound intimate, raw and almost uncomfortable in places, because the thought itself can be uncomfortable if I allow it to become personal.

The line that probably means the most to me in this version is “I have learned how pride can hide itself inside sincerity.” Paul had been sincere. He had been zealous. He had been absolutely convinced that what he was doing was right, and that makes his example much harder for me to dismiss as simply the story of an arrogant man who eventually learned some humility. Sincerity doesn’t protect me from being wrong, and even good motives don’t necessarily mean that pride hasn’t found somewhere to hide inside them.

If the first arrangement was intended to make the thought difficult for me to forget, this one was intended to leave the thought exposed, with very little for it—or me—to hide behind. The same lyrics feel different to me when presented this way. They become less like a lesson about Paul and more like an uncomfortable question about myself: what am I wearing that I have mistaken for part of who I ought to be?

Strip away the pride I carried, every title, every claim.
