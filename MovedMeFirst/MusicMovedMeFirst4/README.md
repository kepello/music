_Music Moved Me First (4)_

This version was a joke.

Unfortunately, the joke landed on me.

A friend particularly liked the original version of Music Moved Me First. He asked me for an MP3 so he could play it in his car.

There was just one problem.

He is not a fan of country music.

So naturally, I decided the appropriate thing to do was give him exactly what he didn’t ask for.

I set out to arrange the song in a way I was reasonably certain he would despise. Fast. Twangy. About 180 beats per minute. Clean lead guitar constantly answering the vocal. A powerful female voice going from soft and breathy to full-throttle wails.

Everything I imagined he might consider sacrilegious to a song he actually liked.

It was terribly fun to make.

It was even more fun to send to him.

And I was right.

Too fast.
Too twangy.

Mission accomplished.

Except I had spent enough time working on the joke that something unfortunate happened.

I started to like it.

Then I started to really like it.

The speed gives the song an excitement none of the other versions have. The call-and-response between the voice and guitar turns the lyric into an actual musical conversation—which, accidentally, is rather perfect for a song about learning to answer music back.

And then there’s the Telecaster.

I own one and love it, though not particularly because of country music. I love its simplicity. There’s something about picking it up that lets me stop searching for the perfect tone and just disappear into playing.

This arrangement has that same effect on me.

It doesn’t brood like the first version. It doesn’t touch my heart in quite the same way as the unplugged version. It doesn’t have the silky groove and swagger I discovered in neo-soul.

It just makes me smile.

Every time it comes on, my toes start tapping. The guitar answers the singer. The singer pushes back. The whole thing races along with absolutely no concern for the fact that it was never supposed to be taken seriously.

Which creates a rather fitting ending to my four experiments with this song.

Music Moved Me First began as a song about discovering that I didn’t have to remain a passive listener. I could touch music, shape it, experiment with it and see where it took me.

So I did.

Four times.

And on the last one, I set out to make something my friend wouldn’t like…

…and accidentally made something I did.

The joke is on the album.
