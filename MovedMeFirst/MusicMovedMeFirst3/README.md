_Music Moved Me First (3)_

This version happened almost by accident.

I had just helped a friend arrange one of his poems to music, and when I asked what he pictured, he said “neo-soul.”

I didn’t even know what that meant.

It wasn’t simply a style I hadn’t worked with—I couldn’t have told you what neo-soul sounded like. So before I could help him, I had some homework to do.

I started digging. Listening. Experiencing a style of music that was completely new to me.

And I was hooked.

There was this groove and swagger that I found infectious. At the same time, there was something strangely familiar about it—a throwback to the ‘70s jazz and funk sounds I’ve always loved, fused with strong hip-hop influences and an organic quality that somehow keeps the whole thing from feeling over-produced.

One serving wasn’t enough.

I had to keep tasting this silky wonderfulness.

And eventually the obvious question occurred to me: Could Music Moved Me First live here?

It felt like a stretch.

Turns out, yes.

To my delight, the song slipped into that groove as though it had been hiding there all along.

And something interesting happened. The lyrics didn’t change, but their personality did. Phrases landed differently. The groove put emphasis in places I hadn’t noticed before. What had sounded searching and almost volatile in the first version, and deeply personal in the unplugged version, suddenly had confidence and swagger.

Music moved me first
But I’m reaching too.

In this arrangement, I really believe that second line.

I’m not just discovering that I’m allowed to participate anymore. I’m enjoying it.

That makes this version particularly appropriate to the song itself. Music Moved Me First is about discovering that I can reach toward music instead of only waiting for it to reach me. And here I was, doing exactly that—wandering into a style I didn’t know, listening until I began to understand it, and then asking what might happen if I brought my own song along.

I really love this rendition.

It pulls different flavors and thoughts out of the same words, in all the right places.

And I can’t think of a better reason to keep experimenting.

Or to keep listening.
