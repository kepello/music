_I'm Patient_

This song started with a different title: Rearranged. The company I worked for had been through another in a string of upper-management changes, and once again the pieces had been moved around. I had a new boss. In practical terms, the change felt like a demotion.

He didn’t know me or my work. He was intelligent and reasonable, and I had no particular reason to think we couldn’t work well together. But I’d been through this before. Each time management changed, I had to rebuild. Over time, I would establish credibility and trust. That trust gave me the freedom and authority to get things done. I genuinely liked the work, and I knew I could build all of that again.

I just didn’t want to. My new boss was occupied with other things and had little time for me. Meanwhile, I found myself doing work that, in my estimation, wasn’t the best use of my time or abilities. I knew the process ahead. Spend the next several months demonstrating what I could do. Explain why I worked the way I did. Earn trust. Gradually regain autonomy.

Train the boss. Again.

That’s where the hook came from:

I’m tired of starting over,
Tired of the same old climb.

But there was another realization underneath the frustration: I wasn’t actually starting from zero. The organization could rearrange around me. Titles and reporting structures could change. A new manager might not know what I had done. But none of that erased what I had learned, what I could do, or the experience I’d accumulated getting there.

Not from zero, don’t erase my gain.
Progress stays even in the rain.

Around that time, a former coworker I had truly enjoyed working with happened to reach out. That opened a door. I began interviewing with his company. The process was extensive, and it took time. There were no guarantees. And that’s when Rearranged became a song about something else.

Patience. I realized there was no emergency. I didn’t have to storm out. I didn’t have to force a decision. I didn’t need to turn frustration into anger just to make myself move. Won’t burn it down just to heat my soul. I could simply wait.

If this opportunity worked out, wonderful. If it didn’t, I could try somewhere else. In the meantime, I could keep doing my job and let things unfold. That is the moment this song records. It’s the strange space where you’ve already decided you’re leaving, but you don’t know where you’re going.

Or when. There was uncertainty in that, but surprisingly little fear.

So I wait — not weak, not slow,
Calculated patience in control.

Eventually, the opportunity did work out. I took the job, and I enjoy the work immensely. But the song isn’t really about that outcome. I didn’t know the ending when I wrote it. It’s about the period before the ending—when nothing was settled except my own decision that I didn’t have to solve everything today.

The ground could shift. I could still stand. And for once, instead of immediately rebuilding what someone else had rearranged, I could simply say: I’m patient. I’ll wait.
