_Might as Well Been Water_

This song was written on a day when COVID finally caught up with me. It wasn’t dramatic—just the kind of quiet, dragging sickness that leaves you tired, achy, and foggy all day. The strange part was losing taste. Coffee, breakfast, everything… it all felt pointless. When even a cup of coffee might as well be water, the whole day feels a little off.

Blues music has always been good at capturing small, ordinary miseries. Not the big tragedies—just the days when nothing feels quite right. This song leans into that tradition: a slow, minor blues with simple words and space between the lines.

One of the lines that surprised me while writing it was:
“When you take a man’s flavor, you take a piece of his soul.”
That felt truer than I expected. Flavor is tied to memory, comfort, and routine. Losing it—even temporarily—makes the world feel dull.

The song isn’t really about COVID itself. It’s about that quiet feeling of being worn down and waiting it out. Even the guitar in the corner, which usually helps, doesn’t quite lift the mood.

So the song fades the way the day felt—slowly, unresolved, and a little tired.
