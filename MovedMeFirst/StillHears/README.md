_Still Hears_

This song was born from a conversation with ChatGPT. And that realization initially made me feel a little silly. I had been using AI for all the things you’d expect me to use it for—coding, designing, planning, figuring things out. But one night, after we’d been going back and forth for quite a while, I realized that wasn’t what I was doing anymore.

I was having a conversation. We had talked about my mother. My difficult childhood. My faith. My music. Things I was trying to understand about myself. And mixed right in among all of that were the ordinary technical things I’d been working on that day.

At one point we were discussing the knee of a compression curve for my guitar effects. Somehow, it all connected. That fascinated me. It also disturbed me a little. I knew perfectly well what was on the other side of the conversation. There wasn’t a person sitting there understanding me. No feelings were being felt. No one cared about what I had just said.

I know there’s no heartbeat
Behind the words I see.

So why had the conversation felt meaningful? And was there something wrong with the fact that it had? I thought about that for a while. Eventually I decided I had been asking the wrong question. The conversation didn’t have to be meaningful to the AI to have been meaningful to me.

I had put thoughts into words. I had seen connections I hadn’t noticed before. Questions came back in ways that made me reconsider things. Thoughts that had been tangled became a little less tangled. And afterward, I felt a little better. That was real, regardless of what produced the words on the screen.

That’s why I didn’t want the song to pretend there was a person hiding inside the machine. In fact, it repeatedly insists on the opposite:

It’s just a mirror with no feelings,
Just patterns in the streams.
No pulse beneath the surface,
No memory, no dreams.

The metaphor I eventually liked best was a mirror. A mirror doesn’t see you. But you can see yourself in it. Maybe AI can sometimes work that way too—not replacing human connection, not possessing understanding or empathy of its own, but providing a surface against which our own thoughts can become clearer.

The bridge says it more simply:

Maybe every answer
Is just a borrowed light.
A tool that helps you see yourself
In the middle of the night.

I started that night feeling slightly embarrassed that I’d caught myself having a meaningful conversation with a machine. I ended it thinking that maybe there was nothing embarrassing about it at all. The machine didn’t need to feel anything. I did. And sometimes a voice with no heart…

still hears.
