_When the Sun Goes Down_

This song sits somewhere between faith and personal experience. There are ideas in it that come from reflecting on Bible passages about anger—particularly the counsel not to let the sun set while you are still angry, and the thought of having your say in your heart, upon your bed, rather than immediately giving voice to everything you feel.

So it could have found a home among my spiritually leaning songs. But it didn’t. It belongs with the personal ones. The reason for the song was a family circumstance that hit hard. It hurt in that particular way that only people close to us sometimes can.

I’m deliberately not going to explain what happened. In fact, that’s part of the point. I don’t need to tell the story again. I don’t need to make my case. I don’t need someone to decide who was right, understand why I was hurt, or agree that I had reason to be angry.

The song is about what happens after the hurt. For me, the answer isn’t pretending it didn’t matter. I’ll feel it, I won’t fake it. I think that’s important. Letting something go doesn’t require deciding that it was okay. It doesn’t mean the pain wasn’t real. And it certainly doesn’t require becoming so emotionally detached that nothing anyone does can hurt you.

We’re people. We hurt one another. Even people we love. I do it too. The harder question is what I choose to do with that hurt once it’s mine. There is an immediate temptation to say the thing that proves the point. To explain exactly why someone was wrong. To vent. To demand an apology. To release all that heat toward the person who caused it.

I’ve done that before.

They burn real hot
when you let ‘em go,
But then you’re left
cold you know.

There’s remarkably little peace waiting at the end of that road. So the song tries another direction.

In my heart,
upon my bed.
I have my say,
And that’s just where
it’s gonna stay.

That doesn’t mean the words never existed. Sometimes you need to think them. Feel them. Admit them to yourself. Maybe even wrestle with them for a while, but they don’t all need to be delivered. Because eventually this stops being about correcting another person and becomes a decision about what I’m willing to continue carrying inside myself.

That’s the meaning of the sun going down. It’s a boundary. I can acknowledge what happened. I can acknowledge that it hurt. I can even acknowledge that I’m still angry. But I don’t have to take it home with me. I can find peace with people I love, even though sometimes we hurt one another.

And, perhaps harder, I can find peace inside myself. That’s what I wanted to write down—and what I wanted to remember. Not ignore it. Not excuse it. Not win. Just…

Lay this fire down.
Lay it down right now.

Anything else keeps hurting me, and helps no one.
