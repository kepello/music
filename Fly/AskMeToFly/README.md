_You Don't Ask Me To Fly_

This song grew out of Isaiah 40:28–31, especially the promise that those hoping in Jehovah will regain power: they will soar on wings like eagles, they will run and not grow weary, they will walk and not tire out.

Those are beautiful words. But when you’re already tired, sometimes even beautiful promises can sound like expectations.

I should be soaring.
I should be running.
I should be stronger than this.

That wasn’t what I wanted this song to say.

What struck me instead was what comes just before the soaring and the running: “He gives power to the tired one and full might to those lacking strength.”

The tired person isn’t being criticized for being tired. The person who has nothing left isn’t being told to try harder. Jehovah knows exactly how much strength is there, and he supplies what is needed.

That became the heart of the song:

You don’t ask me to fly
When I can barely stand.

Maybe someday is the day for flying. Maybe someday is the day for running. But today, faithfulness might look like taking one more step. Sometimes it might simply mean standing.

And that can be enough.

The song isn’t really about lowering expectations. It’s about understanding where the strength comes from in the first place. Jehovah’s power doesn’t begin where ours ends because we finally failed. It was never dependent on our strength.

That’s why the bridge became so important to me:

Someday I’ll rise on wings I don’t have now
Someday I’ll run and not slow down
But today is just a quieter kind of way
Just enough strength to stay.

And ultimately, that’s where the song finds its peace.

I will fly.
I will run.
But I can wait.

Because for today, I don’t need tomorrow’s strength.

I only need enough for the next step.
