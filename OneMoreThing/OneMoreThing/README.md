_One More Thing_

This song started, appropriately enough, while I was doing something that should have been simple. I was working on my guitars, making a few improvements and adjustments, and one thing led to another until at one point I had four guitars and three of them were in some state of disassembly. I remember thinking that they had all actually worked when I started, which seemed both funny and a pretty good description of how I sometimes approach things.

The more I thought about it, though, the less this seemed to be a song about guitars. I do the same thing everywhere. Changing a lightbulb can become an opportunity to rethink the home automation, which leads to switches, locks, cameras, fans and whatever else becomes part of the larger system I can suddenly see in my head. I can usually explain exactly why the more complicated solution is ultimately better, while my wife can quite reasonably ask whether the light switch could just work. Both of us have a point.

The line that really unlocked the song for me was “I can make the hard things simple, make the simple things… less so.” I like complicated problems and I especially like understanding how all of the pieces fit together. Once I see that there is a larger system hiding behind a small problem, it’s difficult for me not to follow it. One question exposes another, which exposes another, and somewhere along the way I’ve disappeared down the rabbit hole. From the outside it can look like I’ve turned a simple project into an unnecessarily complicated one. From inside my head, though, there’s something elegant in there and I just need to see the whole thing.

I also didn’t want the song to turn into an apology for unfinished projects, because that isn’t really how I feel about them. “I may never quite get finished, that’s not what I’m looking for. Give me something worth exploring—I’ll come back wanting more.” That’s probably closer to the truth. I enjoy figuring things out, learning something I didn’t know, finding connections and discovering that the original question was actually part of a much more interesting one. Finishing is nice too, of course, and occasionally even happens.

Musically I wanted the song to have the same slightly manic but playful energy as the subject. The vocal is warm and conversational at first, becoming increasingly animated as the ideas and projects pile up, while the chorus settles into a catchy rhythmic phrasing that makes “one more thing” feel almost inevitable. I also left brief pauses around some of the punchlines because the humor works better when the realization gets a moment to sit there. I didn’t want it performed as a comedy song so much as a perfectly sincere explanation from someone who doesn’t entirely understand why everyone else is laughing.

The outro is probably the most accurate part. After spending an entire song recognizing the pattern, there is no great lesson learned, no promise to simplify my life and no newfound commitment to leaving well enough alone. There’s just a pause, followed by one more thought:

“You know… I’ve been thinking about stainless frets.”

And immediately, we’re off again.
