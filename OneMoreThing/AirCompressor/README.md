_Sleeping with an Air Compressor_

I wrote “Sleeping With an Air Compressor” during a stretch when sleep had become something I thought about far more than I actually did. I have sleep apnea, and the solution is supposedly pretty simple: wear the CPAP consistently, get used to it, sleep better, and eventually feel amazing. At least that is what every doctor, technician, instruction manual, and well-rested person who has ever talked to me about one seems to say. I believe them, at least in the abstract, but at three in the morning with straps around my head, a hose attached to my face, and a machine steadily blowing air up my nose, I sometimes have a somewhat different perspective.

There is something inherently funny to me about all the equipment we willingly attach to ourselves in an effort to get a good night’s sleep. You put on a mask, tighten the straps, connect the hose, turn on the electronics and pressurized air, get everything situated just right, and then you’re supposed to relax and drift peacefully off to sleep. The leaf blower, air compressor, and aviation equipment in the song are obviously exaggerations, but when you’re lying there wide awake at three A.M., they really don’t feel like much of an exaggeration.

So the song is partly me complaining about CPAP, because I certainly do plenty of that, but mostly it’s laughing at the absurdity of the whole situation and what it is like the next day when it hasn’t worked particularly well. I’m walking around on five hours of sleep, drinking far too much coffee, forgetting why I walked into rooms, telling people I’m doing “pretty good, I guess,” while continuing to hear that if I just stick with it, I’ll feel better any day now.

And I do actually believe that. The doctor is probably right, the machine probably does help, and eventually I may even get used to sleeping with the thing.

But at three A.M.—that’s not what I’d say.
