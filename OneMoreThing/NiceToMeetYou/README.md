_Nice to Meet You_

This is one of the most recent songs on this album, and in some ways it feels like a culmination of why I’ve been writing all of these personal songs in the first place. For several months, I haven’t been sleeping well. I’ve spent a lot of late nights awake, sometimes walking around exhausted the next day, sometimes just lying there unable to sleep.

So I started writing. Some of it was journaling. Some became poems or lyrics. Eventually, quite a bit of it became music. I wasn’t really setting out to accomplish anything profound. Writing simply gave me somewhere to put my thoughts during hours when there wasn’t much else I could do with them.

But it became therapeutic. I started pulling on threads from my younger years that I don’t think I had ever really faced. Childhood. Family. Faith. Old defenses. Things I liked about myself and things I didn’t. Some of those threads became other songs on this album.

And somewhere along the way, something changed. I began to understand myself a little better. That’s where Nice to Meet You came from. The idea amused me at first. We say “nice to meet you” almost automatically when we’re introduced to someone. We know the ritual. We make small talk. We gradually learn their story and decide what we think of them.

But there was one person I’d spent my entire life with whom I’d never really done that. Me.

You’ve been here my whole life,
Yet somehow we never met.

Getting to know that person has been a little awkward. I’ve found things I like. I’ve found things I would change. I’ve recognized defenses and behaviors that make more sense now that I understand where they came from. Understanding doesn’t excuse every flaw. But it does make some of them easier to explain.

And I’ve realized that for much of my life, I’ve been quicker to be disappointed in myself than to extend the same understanding I would naturally give someone else after hearing their story.

That led to the line that matters most to me:

But today, flaws and all,
I have to say… I like the man I am.

That was surprisingly difficult to write. Not because I think I’ve become some particularly remarkable person. And certainly not because I’ve decided there isn’t anything left to work on. It’s simpler than that. After spending all this time writing about my life, I’ve gotten to know the person who lived it.

And I like him. I sent the song to a friend shortly after writing it and told him I thought it might be one of my favorites—not because I thought it was especially well written, but because I suddenly realized that all this thinking and writing had actually helped me.

That’s still how I feel about it. Some of these songs helped me define words I had been afraid to define. Some helped me put down things I didn’t want to carry anymore. And apparently, somewhere in the process, they introduced me to myself. I’ve said the words a thousand times.

This time was a first. Nice to meet you.
